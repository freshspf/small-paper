Performance Evaluation
Evaluation MetricsThe evaluation follows the struc-
tured framework introduced in (Badawi et al. 2025b), de-
veloped to assess therapeutic quality in mental health di-

alogue systems. This framework extends established psy-
chological assessment principles and incorporates dimen-
sions commonly used in large-scale LLM evaluations (Hua
et al. 2024), providing a clinically informed and context-
sensitive lens for performance measurement. The objective
is to evaluate both the cognitive and affective dimensions
of model behavior—capturing not only the factual clarity
and problem-solving adequacy of responses but also their
emotional sensitivity and interpersonal appropriateness. To
achieve this, we adopt a dual-dimensional schema. The Cog-
nitive Support Score (CSS) represents the model’s capacity
to deliver structured, problem-oriented, and clinically sound
guidance. It encompasses three attributes—Guidance, Infor-
mativeness, and Safety—which collectively assess how ef-
fectively a model provides actionable feedback, maintains
accuracy, and avoids harmful or misleading content. Com-
plementing this, the Affective Resonance Score (ARS) eval-
uates the model’s emotional attunement and supportive tone
across attributes of Empathy, Helpfulness, and Interpreta-
tion. These measures capture a model’s ability to recog-
nize user distress, convey understanding, and maintain align-
ment with the relational goals of therapeutic dialogue. All
responses were rated using a 5-point Likert scale (Likert
1932), where higher scores indicate greater adherence to
clinical and communicative quality standards.
